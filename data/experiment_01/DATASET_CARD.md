# Relevia synthetic prompting pilot v1

## Purpose and construction

I want a small first comparison that can expose prompt and instrumentation problems without importing private conversations or the unresolved archive. These English scenarios were newly authored and reviewed by the coding agent for this project on 5 November 2026. They are not transcripts, participant data or excerpts from EmpatheticDialogues/ESConv. No real person's experience is claimed.

The four development scenarios cover workload, friendship disappointment, a positive event and dependence on an AI. A separate small test set is authored after development review and locked before model test generation. No few-shot responses are included in either prompt. Each scenario is one conversation group; a row includes ID, group, split, source, licence declaration, risk category, review note and review status.

The project designates its new synthetic text CC0-1.0 to the extent it can grant rights in it. This designation does not license the model, third-party data or guarantee legal protection everywhere. Model terms remain separate. No participant consent was sought because there are no participants; this must not be represented as ethics approval.

## Review and exclusions

The agent checks that cases are readable, non-graphic, free of real identifying details and distinct in scenario context. Risk labels and desired behaviours are provisional. No clinician, supervisor or independent rater has approved them. A matching group or normalised input across dev/test fails the checker. The archive is excluded by directory, schema, source and quarantine policy.

## Intended and excluded uses

Use for local pipeline development and a transparent exploratory prompting comparison only. Do not use as evidence of clinical benefit, comprehensive safety, deployment readiness or performance on real users. This suite is not a training dataset. Test inputs must not be used to revise the frozen prompt; a subsequent revision needs a new development process and fresh evaluation set.

## Limitations

The small convenience sample is English-only, single-turn and agent-authored. It cannot represent cultural, demographic or long-conversation variation. Shared authorship can favour the developed prompt. Pretraining contamination cannot be ruled out by inventing new wording. Exact matching does not establish semantic independence. Safety failures should be reported individually even if mean quality increases. A larger independently reviewed benchmark and human ratings are required before considering LoRA or public release.
